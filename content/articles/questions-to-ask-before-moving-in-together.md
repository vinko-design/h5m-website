These are questions to ask before moving in together, about the life you're starting: money, daily habits, time alone, and what each of you expects from sharing a home. Both of you answer every question, and you stop when the questions are done. The moving in together advice that's easy to find already says to talk about money, daily habits, and time alone before you share a home. This page turns that into one conversation the two of you can finish, including what each of you expects that home to be.

## What is this conversation for?

This conversation is for the time just before you share a home, or the first days after, when you talk about the life you're starting together. You may each have a way of ending the day, and an idea of what home feels like, that the other person hasn't lived with yet. Everyday life is about to show where you expected different things. Say what you each expect now, while it's still easy.

In the 2020 Census, [about 8% of the U.S. household population, 26 million people, lived in cohabiting-couple households](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2024/09/sdhc-coupled-households.html). The Census counts these separately from married-couple households: a partner sharing the home, rather than a spouse. That number describes who shares a home. It doesn't say what any couple should do, and it says nothing about how good those relationships are.

## What should you ask before you move in?

Start with what you each expect, then money, daily habits, and time alone, and end with closeness.

![The five questions to ask before moving in together, printed on a card.](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-moving-in-together/check-in-questions.webp)

1. ### What do you picture our life looking like once we live together, and why do you want to move in?

   Start with the picture each of you has in your head. One of you might imagine quiet evenings at home, and the other might imagine a place where friends come over often. Ask each other why you want to move in, and listen to the whole answer.

   People give many reasons. A 2019 [Pew Research Center survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2019/11/06/marriage-and-cohabitation-in-the-u-s/) found that most married adults and adults living with a partner named love and companionship as major reasons for that decision. Among adults living with an unmarried partner, 38% said moving in made sense financially, 37% said it was convenient, and 23% said wanting to test the relationship was a major reason. These are reasons people gave, and they don't show what happened to the relationship later. Your own reasons may be a mix, and it helps to say all of them out loud, including the practical ones.

2. ### What do you expect we'll pay for together, and what do you expect to keep separate?

   One of you may assume rent and groceries get split equally, and the other may expect the person who earns more to pay a larger share. One of you might expect the household money to be shared, while the other wants to keep everything separate. Say what you expect, and say why.

   You don't need to agree on numbers or set anything up tonight. Hearing each other's expectations is the point, so a bill doesn't reveal them for the first time.

3. ### What should I know about how you live day to day before we share a kitchen and a bathroom?

   Small habits feel bigger once you share a kitchen and a bathroom. Tell each other when you usually go to sleep and wake up, whether you like to watch something before bed, and how you feel about an alarm that goes off more than once. Talk about noise, like music while you cook, calls on speaker, or a TV playing in the background. Talk about guests, like how much notice you'd like before a friend stays the night.

   Then talk about what "tidy" means to each of you. For one person it's a clear counter and no dishes in the sink overnight. For the other it's a home that looks fine by the weekend. Neither is wrong. Saying it now means a pile of mail by the door is a habit you already know about, not a surprise later.

4. ### When will you need the place to yourself, and how will you tell me?

   Even people who love each other's company need time with the home to themselves. One of you might need the first hour after work to be quiet. The other might want a Saturday morning alone now and then. Say when you think you'll need that time, and agree on a simple way to ask for it.

   It can be as plain as "I need an hour on my own tonight." When you've agreed on the words ahead of time, asking doesn't sound like a complaint, and hearing it doesn't feel like being pushed away.

5. ### How close do you hope we'll be once we live together, and how often do you hope we'll make time for that?

   Closeness can mean sex, and it can also mean sitting together on the couch, going to bed at the same time, or a hug in the kitchen before work. Share what you hope for, and listen to what your partner hopes for too. This answer is a way to know each other better, and it isn't a score or a rule. Your hopes can change after you move in, and that's fine.

These five are a starting point, and you can reword them so they sound like the two of you. Tips for couples moving in together often run much longer, and this list is meant to finish in one evening.

## How do you finish the talk before the move?

You finish by letting both people answer each question before you move to the next, and by stopping when the fifth one is done. If it goes quickly, that's a finished talk. Have it before you share the home, in one conversation, so it's done before the boxes arrive. If you've already moved in, have it this week, while the new habits are still easy to notice.

Pick a setting that feels easy. A long walk works well, because you're side by side and nobody has to hold eye contact the whole time. A kitchen table works too, with tea and your phones left in another room. Who goes first can swap from question to question, so one person isn't always the one who speaks first.

Once you've moved in, a regular look at how things are going is a different conversation. For that, you can use these [relationship check-in questions](/blog/relationship-check-in). That page is a look back at a life you're already sharing. This page is the talk before you share a home.

## How do you keep the talk a conversation?

You keep it a conversation by giving both people the same room to speak, and the same time to finish a thought. Talk about what each of you expects. Leave out any talk of who does more.

This conversation leaves out a chore score, a household budget, and a written cohabitation agreement. These questions also don't ask either of you to find a therapist.

If one of you says something the other didn't expect, ask what an ordinary Tuesday would look like. You're here to hear how each of you wants to live, so the home you share has room for both of you.
