Good topics to talk about with your partner range from light ones, like what made you laugh this week, to serious ones, like where you'd like your life together to be in a few years. Pick two or three together, take turns answering, and follow whichever thread interests you most. Either of you can skip a topic or stop whenever you like, and nobody keeps score.

You might be here because your conversations have settled into the same few subjects, like the car that needs a service and what you're doing on Saturday. Or you might be here because talking used to come without any effort, and lately you sit down together and aren't sure what to say. Those are two different situations, and a few good topics can help with either one.

## What can a few good topics do for the two of you?

On an ordinary week, a topic gives you a reason to talk about something other than plans and errands. You hear how your partner thinks about a trip, a memory, or the next few years, and they hear the same from you. Even after a long time together, there's usually something you've never asked about, simply because the subject never came up.

If your talk has narrowed to the same few subjects, a topic from outside that circle gives you something new to share. A question about a childhood holiday or a skill you'd love to learn can take an ordinary dinner somewhere neither of you expected.

If talking has lost some of its ease for you, having a topic ready gives you a place to begin. You don't have to think up something interesting on the spot. You only have to pick a subject and see where it goes.

Talking like this is one way to look after your relationship. It's worth doing on a week when everything feels fine, and it's also a gentle way to reach for each other when things have felt a little distant.

## How do you choose topics together?

Read through the topics below together, and each of you picks one that sounds good. That gives you two to start with, which is plenty for one conversation. If you have a long evening or a long drive ahead, you can each pick another.

Choosing together matters because it means neither of you is answering a list the other person set. If one of you picks something light and the other picks something serious, start with the light one. It gets you both talking before you move to a subject that needs more thought.

If you're the one who wants to start, you could say something like, "I found a list of things couples can talk about. Want to each pick one while we cook tonight?" Your partner might want to pick right away and start talking. They might suggest the weekend instead, when neither of you is tired. Either answer is a good start. If they'd rather not right now, that's a fair answer too, and you can suggest it another time.

Next time, swap who picks first, or let one person choose both topics while the other chooses next time.

## Which topics can you talk about with your partner?

These six go from light and fun to serious, with time in the middle for what you're grateful for and where you come from. Each one is a subject, not a single question, so reword it until it sounds like the two of you. Both of you answer each topic you choose.

1. ### Something that made you laugh this week

   Maybe it was a story a coworker told, a video a friend sent, or the dog running off with a sock. A topic like this is for enjoying a few minutes together, and that's reason enough. If you both like where it goes, try the funniest thing that ever happened to you on a holiday. Keep it about laughing together, and leave teasing each other out of it.

2. ### A trip you'd love to take together

   Start big: if you had a free month and no limits, where would you go? Then try a smaller version, like a town an hour's drive away that neither of you has visited. Dreaming about a trip is fun on its own. Sometimes it turns into a real plan, and sometimes it stays a nice thing to imagine, and both are fine.

3. ### Something you'd like to learn or try

   It might be a pottery class, baking bread, learning to swim properly, or picking up some Spanish before a trip. Your answers might point at something to do together. Or they might show you a goal your partner would love some encouragement with, like the guitar they keep meaning to pick up again.

4. ### Something you've been grateful for lately

   Each of you names something from your own life that you're glad about right now. It could be a good night's sleep, a friend who called at the right moment, a walk in the sun, or something your partner did. Saying these out loud gives the good parts of your week a moment of attention that busy days often skip.

5. ### A memory from growing up

   Talk about how your family spent holidays, a teacher you still think about, your first job, or what dinner looked like at home. These stories often explain small things about how each of you lives now, like why one of you cares so much about eating together on Sundays. Some memories are light and some are heavier. Whoever is sharing decides how much to say.

6. ### Where you'd like your life together to be in a few years

   This is the serious one. Talk about where you'd like to live, how you'd like work to fit into your days, whether you want children, or a money aim such as saving for a place with a garden. Your answers might not match, and that's useful to know. You're two people telling each other something true, and nothing has to be decided tonight.

## How do you keep it a conversation?

Take turns, and swap who answers first with each topic, so one of you isn't always opening up while the other waits. While your partner talks, your only job is to follow what they're saying. When they finish, ask about one detail, like "What was it about that teacher?" or "What would a weekend in that town look like?" A question like that shows you were listening, and it often brings out the part they most wanted to tell you.

Follow one thread instead of moving quickly through the list. If the trip topic turns into twenty minutes about the summer you both spent working at the same café, stay there. You can leave the other topic for another day.

Either of you can skip a topic, or stop the whole conversation, without giving a reason. Saying "I'd rather leave that one for now" should feel normal, and hearing it shouldn't lead to a debate. If your partner doesn't want to talk about something, let them know you'd be glad to whenever they want, and leave the choice with them.

Nobody keeps score. Different answers are two people describing their own lives, and nobody is ahead or behind. What your partner shares belongs to them, so keep it out of later disagreements. That way, both of you feel free to say more next time.

## When and where do these topics fit?

Use them wherever you already spend time together. One topic over dinner works well. So does a single topic while you wait for a table at a restaurant or fold laundry on a Sunday afternoon.

Longer occasions leave room for several. A long drive, an evening walk, or a date night can hold two or three topics with plenty of time for side threads. Side by side, in a car or on a walk, some of the deeper subjects come more naturally, because nobody has to look across a table the whole time.

Doing something together can also start a conversation by itself. Playing a card game, cooking a new recipe, or painting a room gives you something to talk around, and a topic can slip in during the quiet stretches.

## How do you know when a conversation is finished?

A light chat is finished when you've talked. If you laughed about the dog and the sock and then moved on to what's for dessert, that was a good conversation. You don't need to draw a lesson from it.

A deeper thread sometimes leaves you with something you both want. Talking about the next few years, you might realize you'd both like to spend more weekends outdoors. When that happens, you can close by naming one thing you'd like to pay attention to together, something small enough to actually happen, like a hike before the end of the month. That's one way to end a serious talk. It isn't the point of every conversation.

When that small thing does happen, notice it together. Say how much you enjoyed it. That's a high-five moment, and you can celebrate it however suits you, with an actual high five, a good lunch afterwards, or just a smile. If the month gets busy and the hike doesn't happen, say so, choose another weekend, and try again.
